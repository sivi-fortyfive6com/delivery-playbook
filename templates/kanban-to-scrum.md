# Kanban → Scrum, in Plain Language

**Kanban** is a conveyor belt: work flows through steps continuously, and you limit how much is on the belt at once.
**Scrum** is a series of short trips: every 2 weeks the team commits to a set of work, delivers it, and reviews it.

## When to switch

| Signal | Stay on Kanban | Move to Scrum |
|---|---|---|
| Work type | Mostly unplanned (support, requests) | Mostly planned (projects, features) |
| Predictability | Hard to plan more than a few days ahead | Can plan 2 weeks ahead |
| Board health | WIP still unstable | Flow is visible and stable |
| Stakeholders | Want quick turnaround on items | Want regular, demonstrable progress |

## Transition steps

1. **Stabilise Kanban.** Keep the board current, set WIP limits, hold a daily stand-up.
2. **Build the backlog.** Groom it with the business owner and estimate roughly.
3. **Run a trial sprint.** Plan two weeks, hold a review and a retro, then adjust.
4. **Protect support.** Hold back a share of capacity, or rotate one person, for unplanned work.
5. **Add ceremonies one at a time.** Don't introduce all of them in week one.

| Ceremony | Purpose | Time-box (2-week sprint) |
|---|---|---|
| Sprint planning | Agree what we'll deliver | 2 hrs |
| Daily stand-up | Unblock each other | 15 min |
| Sprint review | Show working results to the business | 1 hr |
| Retrospective | Improve how we work | 1 hr |
